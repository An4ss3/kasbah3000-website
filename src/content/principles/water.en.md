In a context of water stress, Kasbah3000 combines efficient fixtures, leak detection, rainwater collection and low-water landscaping. Usage is monitored to understand demand and reduce losses.

Greywater can be treated through appropriate systems and reused for irrigation in line with health standards. Residents and partners jointly track quality and volumes to continually improve the local water cycle.

## Rainwater harvesting

Rainwater harvesting means collecting and storing rainwater from rooftops, paved areas and open land surfaces. It reduces dependence on groundwater and municipal supply while providing a free, renewable source for domestic use, irrigation and aquaponic systems.

Morocco's seasonal rains, particularly in rural areas, offer immense potential for capture. With proper storage, a single kasbah could meet most of its non-potable water needs year-round — crucial in regions where scarcity is growing through desertification and climate change.

In a kasbah the system works in sequence: water is collected from rooftops and solar panels, directed through gutters into filters and first flush diverters to ensure cleanliness, and stored in underground cisterns of 50,000–100,000 litres. It is then distributed by gravity or pumps for household use, agroforestry irrigation and ponds, often combined with treated greywater to maximise efficiency.

The harvested water is put to four uses:

- Filtered for domestic uses such as cleaning, bathing and flushing
- Redirected to irrigation for agroforests growing lemons, pomegranates, figs and potatoes
- Used in schwimmteich systems for natural water purification and landscaping
- Integrated into greywater recycling systems, maximising every drop

The available volume follows a simple relationship — rainfall × collection area × efficiency. For 0.3 m of rainfall across a 2,000 m² catchment at 80% efficiency, the annual harvest is 480 m³.

A worked scenario sizes underground cisterns at 100,000 litres: enough to support 30 families and irrigate one hectare of agroforest for over 8 months. It saves nearly half a million litres of municipal or pumped groundwater annually, lowers water costs, supports self-sufficient agroforestry and provides backup water for households during droughts.
